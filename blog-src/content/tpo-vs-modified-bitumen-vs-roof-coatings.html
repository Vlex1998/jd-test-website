When a commercial building needs a new roof, or a better one, the choice usually comes down to a few common low-slope systems. Each has strengths and trade-offs in the Texas climate. Here is how they compare.

TPO (Thermoplastic Polyolefin)

TPO is a single-ply membrane, usually white, that has become one of the most widely used commercial roofing systems.

  • Pros: reflective white surface that reduces heat gain, heat-welded seams that are strong and watertight, relatively cost-effective, and lightweight.
  • Cons: quality and thickness vary between manufacturers, installation quality at seams is critical, and punctures from foot traffic or debris need prompt repair.
  • Best for: warehouses, retail, offices, and buildings where energy efficiency matters.

PVC

PVC is another single-ply, heat-welded membrane, similar to TPO in appearance.

  • Pros: strong chemical and grease resistance, which is useful for restaurants and some industrial buildings, plus durable welded seams.
  • Cons: generally costs more than TPO.

Modified Bitumen

Modified bitumen is an asphalt-based system installed in rolls, often in multiple layers. It may be torch-applied, cold-applied with adhesive, or self-adhered.

  • Pros: multi-layer redundancy, very tough and resistant to foot traffic and punctures, and a long track record.
  • Cons: usually dark unless it has a reflective cap sheet or coating, so it absorbs more heat. Installation can be more labor-intensive.
  • Best for: roofs with heavy foot traffic, smaller low-slope areas, and some multi-family buildings.

Roof Coatings (Silicone, Acrylic)

Coatings are liquid-applied over an existing roof to restore and protect it. Silicone and acrylic are common choices.

  • Pros: can extend the life of a roof in fair condition without a full tear-off, reflective, seamless, and less disruptive to building occupants. Silicone handles ponding water better than acrylic.
  • Cons: not a fix for a failing roof. If the existing roof or insulation is saturated with water, it needs repair or replacement first. Surface preparation is critical.
  • Best for: metal roofs and single-ply or modified bitumen roofs that are aging but still sound.

How to Choose

The right system depends on your building's use, roof condition, foot traffic, budget, and how long you plan to own the property. A core sample or moisture survey can show whether the existing roof can be restored or needs replacement.
