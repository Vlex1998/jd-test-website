The short answer: homeowners insurance covers a roof leak when the leak was caused by a sudden, covered event, like a storm, and does not cover a leak that developed because the roof got old or was not maintained. The leak itself is not what the policy is looking at. The cause is.

Leaks That Are Usually Covered

  • Wind tore off shingles and rain came in through the opening.
  • Hail cracked shingles or a vent and water followed.
  • A tree or limb fell through the roof.
  • Fire, lightning, or vandalism damaged the roof.
  • A sudden accidental event such as debris from a neighbor's roof puncturing yours in a storm.

In these cases, your policy generally pays to repair the roof and the resulting interior damage (ceilings, drywall, insulation, flooring), minus your deductible. In Texas, the wind and hail deductible is usually a percentage of the dwelling coverage, often 1 to 2 percent, which can be several thousand dollars.

Leaks That Are Usually Not Covered

  • Age and wear. Shingles that cracked, curled, or lost granules over 20 years. See signs you need a new roof.
  • Failed pipe boots, dried-out caulk, rusted flashing. These are maintenance items. See pipe boot leaks and chimney flashing leaks.
  • Improper installation. Workmanship problems are the installer's warranty issue, not the insurer's.
  • Clogged gutters or ponding water from neglected drainage.
  • Mold or rot that built up over time from a slow leak you did not address.
  • Flooding. Rising water is excluded from homeowners policies and covered only by flood insurance.

The Gray Area: Storm Plus Old Roof

Most disputes happen when a storm finishes off a roof that was already weak. The adjuster sees wear and tear, you see storm damage, and both are partly right. What tips it one way or the other is evidence: dated photos, weather records, and whether there is clear, fresh storm damage (creased shingles, hail bruises with exposed asphalt, impact marks on soft metals) rather than just general deterioration. Read what to do if your claim is denied for how to make the case.

Interior Damage Can Be Covered Even When the Roof Is Not

Some policies pay for the sudden interior damage from a leak (a collapsed ceiling, ruined flooring) even when the roof repair itself is excluded as wear and tear. It depends on your policy language. Ask your agent specifically about "resulting damage" coverage.

What Your Policy Expects From You

Every homeowners policy requires you to protect the property from further damage after a loss. In practice that means tarping the roof, moving furniture, and drying out wet areas quickly. Keep receipts. These mitigation costs are typically reimbursable. If you let a small leak go for months and the ceiling collapses, the insurer can deny the enlarged damage as neglect. See emergency roof tarping.

Should You File a Claim for a Leak?

Before filing, get the leak diagnosed. If it is a $400 pipe boot replacement, filing a claim with a $4,000 deductible accomplishes nothing except adding a claim to your record. If it is storm damage across the whole roof, the claim is clearly worth it. A local roofer can usually tell you which situation you are in on the first visit.
