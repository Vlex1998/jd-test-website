Roof coatings are the most cost-effective way to extend the life of a commercial roof that is tired but not failed. A fluid-applied coating seals the surface, reflects heat, and adds a renewable waterproof layer for a fraction of replacement cost. The two coatings most used on Texas commercial roofs are silicone and acrylic, and they are not interchangeable. The right choice depends on your roof's drainage, traffic, and budget.

What a Coating Does and Does Not Do

A coating restores the surface of an existing roof. It does not fix wet insulation, failed deck, or structural ponding. Before any coating, the roof needs a moisture survey, repairs to seams and flashings, cleaning, and sometimes a primer. Coating over a roof with wet insulation traps the water and guarantees failure. Silicone Coatings

Strengths:

  • Handles ponding water. Silicone is the only common coating that can sit under standing water indefinitely without degrading. On a Houston flat roof with any ponding, this is the deciding factor.
  • UV stable. Does not chalk or break down in sun; stays flexible for the long term.
  • Fewer coats. High-solids silicone often goes on in one thick coat.
  • Moisture-cure. Cures in humidity, which Houston has plenty of.

Weaknesses:

  • Slippery when wet. A real safety issue on roofs with foot traffic. Walkway pads are needed at service areas.
  • Attracts dirt. Loses reflectivity faster than acrylic unless cleaned.
  • Hard to recoat with anything else. Once a roof is siliconed, only silicone will stick to it in the future.
  • Higher material cost than acrylic.
  • Less abrasion resistant. Can be damaged by dragged equipment or tools.

Acrylic Coatings

Strengths:

  • Lower cost. Typically the least expensive elastomeric coating.
  • Highly reflective and stays cleaner, which helps cooling bills.
  • Easy to recoat with more acrylic or other products.
  • Better foot traffic resistance than silicone.
  • Water-based and low odor, which matters on occupied buildings.

Weaknesses:

  • Does not tolerate ponding water. Standing water softens and eventually washes away acrylic. On a roof that ponds, acrylic will fail in those areas.
  • Weather-sensitive application. Needs dry conditions and warm temperatures to cure, and rain within hours of application can wash it off. Houston's afternoon storms shorten the application window.
  • Multiple coats are usually needed to reach thickness.
  • Loses thickness over time and needs recoating every 10 years or so.

Which One for Your Building

  • Flat roof with any ponding: silicone. No contest.
  • Well-drained roof with heavy HVAC service traffic: acrylic, with walkway pads.
  • Metal roof (sloped, drains well): either works; acrylic is common and economical over metal, silicone is used where seams and fasteners need the most sealing. See metal roofing in Texas.
  • Budget-driven, well-drained roof: acrylic.
  • Long-term, lowest-maintenance: silicone.

Other Coatings

Polyurethane coatings offer the best abrasion and impact resistance and are sometimes used as a base coat under silicone or acrylic on high-traffic roofs. Aluminized asphalt coatings are inexpensive but offer little waterproofing and are mostly a UV protectant for old built-up roofs. Cost and Lifespan

Installed coating costs in Houston commonly run a few dollars per square foot, with silicone at the higher end and prep work being the biggest variable. A properly applied coating adds 10 to 20 years to a sound roof, and most systems can be recoated at the end of that period for less than the original application. Many manufacturers offer 10 to 20 year warranties when applied by an approved contractor at the specified thickness. See commercial roof replacement cost for comparison.

Maintenance After Coating

Coated roofs still need semi-annual inspections, drain cleaning, and prompt repair of any damage from trades working on the roof. See commercial flat roof maintenance in Texas.
