Most roofing estimates are accurate for what can be seen. The surprises come from what cannot be seen until the old roof is removed. Knowing the most common hidden costs ahead of time helps you budget and compare quotes fairly.

1. Rotten or Damaged Decking

The plywood or OSB under your shingles can rot from slow leaks, poor ventilation, or age. It is impossible to fully inspect until tear-off. Ask your roofer for a per-sheet replacement price in writing, so there is no guessing later. In Houston's humidity, some decking replacement on older roofs is common.

2. Extra Layers of Shingles

Some homes have a second layer of shingles installed over the first. Removing two layers takes more labor and adds disposal weight. A good roofer will check for multiple layers during the inspection, but sometimes it is only clear once work begins.

3. Code Upgrades

Building codes change. Items like drip edge, specific underlayment, or ventilation requirements may need to be brought up to current code during a replacement. If you are filing an insurance claim, check whether your policy includes ordinance or law coverage, which can help pay for required upgrades.

4. Flashing and Chimney Work

Old flashing is often reused by low-bid contractors to save money. Replacing step flashing, counter flashing, and chimney flashing costs more upfront but prevents leaks. Make sure your estimate states whether flashing is new or reused.

5. Ventilation Fixes

If your attic does not have enough intake or exhaust ventilation, your roofer may recommend adding soffit vents, ridge vents, or other exhaust. This adds cost but protects your new roof and your shingle warranty. See roof ventilation and attic heat.

6. Gutters and Fascia

Rotted fascia boards behind the gutters are common in Houston. They often are not visible until the gutters are removed. Gutters may also need to be detached and reinstalled during the roof work.

7. Permits and HOA Requirements

Permit fees vary by city. Some HOAs require specific shingle colors or product lines, which can change the price. See HOA rules and roof replacement.

How to Avoid Surprises

  • Get a thorough inspection, including the attic, before you sign.
  • Ask for unit prices for decking and fascia in writing.
  • Make sure the estimate lists every component, not just shingles. Our guide to reading a roofing estimate helps.
  • Keep a small cushion in your budget for hidden repairs.
  • Ask how the roofer will notify you of extra work before doing it.

Beware the lowest bid. A quote that is far below the others often leaves out items that become "change orders" later. Compare what is included, not just the total.
