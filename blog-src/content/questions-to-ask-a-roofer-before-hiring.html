Most roofing problems homeowners end up with were avoidable at the hiring stage. The roofer was uninsured, the crew was a subcontractor nobody vetted, the warranty was verbal, or the contract did not say what shingle was going on. Fifteen questions, asked before you sign, will tell you almost everything you need to know. Here they are, with what a good answer sounds like.

Business and Insurance

1. Are you insured, and can I see the certificate? You want general liability and workers' compensation (or an occupational accident policy for the crew). Ask for the certificate to be sent directly from the insurance agent, not a photocopy. Without workers' comp, an injured worker can come after you. Texas does not require roofers to carry it, so many do not.

2. How long have you been in business under this name, and where is your office? A physical local address, a landline, and a few years of history under one name. Storm chasers reorganize under new names after every season. See local roofer vs storm chaser.

3. Do you have a license? Texas does not require one, so the honest answer is "Texas has no state license, but here is our RCAT license or manufacturer certification." A roofer who claims a Texas state roofing license is either confused or lying. See does Texas require a roofing license.

4. Can you give me three recent local references and a few addresses I can drive past? Recent and local. Not a testimonial page.

The Crew and the Work

5. Who will actually be on my roof? Your own employees, or a subcontracted crew? Subcontracting is common and not necessarily bad, but the company should know the crew, use them regularly, and cover them under its insurance.

6. Will there be a supervisor on site, and who do I call if there is a problem during the job? Get a name and a cell number.

7. Will you pull the permit? Where one is required, the contractor should pull it in their name. A roofer asking you to pull it is avoiding accountability. See roof permits in Houston.

8. How do you handle rotten decking? You want a per-sheet price in writing and a promise to show you the bad wood before replacing it. See hidden costs of roof replacement.

9. Do you tear off down to the deck, or roof over? Tear off. See can you put new shingles over old ones.

Materials

10. Exactly what products are going on my roof? Brand and product name for the shingle, underlayment, ice and water shield, starter, ridge cap, pipe boots, and vents. "Architectural shingles" is not an answer. Read how to read a roofing estimate.

11. Are you certified by the shingle manufacturer? Certification programs (such as Owens Corning Preferred or CertainTeed credentials) mean the installer has been trained and can offer the manufacturer's enhanced warranty. See Owens Corning vs GAF vs CertainTeed.

Warranty and Payment

12. What is your workmanship warranty, in writing, and how long? Separate from the manufacturer's warranty. Ask what it covers and what voids it. See roof warranties explained.

13. What are the payment terms? A deposit at signing or material delivery and the balance at completion is normal. Full payment up front is not. Payment only after you have walked the job and the cleanup is done.

14. If this is an insurance job, how do you handle the deductible? The only correct answer is that you pay it. Any offer to waive, absorb, or "work with you" on the deductible is illegal in Texas. See can a roofer pay your deductible.

15. What does cleanup include? Magnetic nail sweep of the yard and driveway, debris hauled the same day, gutters cleared of tear-off debris, and protection for landscaping and AC units during the job.

Red Flags in the Answers

  • Reluctance to put anything in writing
  • Pressure to sign today for a "storm price"
  • No physical address, or an address that turns out to be a mailbox
  • A contract that assigns your insurance benefits to the roofer
  • Cash-only or large upfront payments
