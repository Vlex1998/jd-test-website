A residential roof is a system of many parts working together, not just the shingles you can see from the street. Here's a breakdown of the components that matter most, whether you're trying to understand a repair estimate or just curious what's actually up there.

The Roof Deck and Structure

  • Rafters or trusses: the structural framework that supports the entire roof.
  • Decking (sheathing): plywood or OSB panels fastened to the rafters that everything else attaches to.
  • Ridge: the horizontal peak where two roof slopes meet at the top.
  • Hip: the external angle where two roof slopes meet, running from ridge to eave.
  • Valley: the internal angle where two slopes meet, channeling water downward.
  • Eave: the lower edge of the roof that overhangs the exterior wall.
  • Rake: the sloped edge of the roof at a gable end.

Waterproofing Layers

  • Underlayment: a water-resistant layer installed over the decking, beneath the shingles.
  • Ice and water shield: a self-adhering waterproof membrane used at vulnerable spots like valleys and eaves.
  • Drip edge: metal flashing along the eaves and rakes that directs water into the gutters and protects the fascia.
  • Starter strip: the first row of shingle material along the eave, sealing the roof's most vulnerable edge.

Flashing and Ventilation

  • Step flashing: layered metal pieces used where a roof meets a wall, such as at a dormer.
  • Chimney flashing: flashing custom-fit around a chimney to prevent leaks.
  • Valley flashing: metal or membrane reinforcement in a roof valley.
  • Pipe boot (vent boot): a rubber and metal collar sealing around plumbing vent pipes.
  • Ridge vent: a ventilation component along the ridge that lets hot attic air escape.
  • Soffit vents: intake vents under the eaves that pull in fresh air to balance attic ventilation.

Finishing Components

  • Field shingles: the main body of shingles covering most of the roof.
  • Ridge cap shingles: specially shaped shingles that finish off the ridge line.
  • Fascia: the vertical board along the roof edge that gutters typically mount to.
  • Gutters and downspouts: the system that collects and channels water away from the roof and foundation.
