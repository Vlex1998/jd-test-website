Walk through any Houston subdivision and nearly every asphalt roof is one of three brands: Owens Corning, GAF, or CertainTeed. All three make good shingles. The differences in their mainstream architectural lines are smaller than the marketing suggests, and what matters far more is the specific product tier, the installer, and the warranty you actually end up with. Here is an honest comparison.

The Mainstream Architectural Lines

  • Owens Corning Duration. Known for the SureNail strip, a reinforced fabric band in the nailing zone that gives a wider target and better nail hold. 130 mph wind rating with the standard nailing pattern. Available in Duration FLEX (Class 4 impact-resistant) and Duration with algae resistance. Owens Corning is the brand we install most.
  • GAF Timberline HDZ. The best-selling shingle in North America. Also uses a reinforced nailing zone (LayerLock) and carries a high wind rating when installed with GAF starter and ridge. Timberline AS II is the Class 4 version.
  • CertainTeed Landmark. A heavier, two-layer laminated shingle that many roofers consider the thickest-feeling of the three at the same price tier. Landmark ClimateFlex is the Class 4 version. CertainTeed also has Landmark PRO and Premium tiers with more weight and longer warranties.

In Houston, we have seen all three perform well when installed right and fail early when installed badly. Brand is not the variable that predicts roof life.

Wind Ratings

All three offer 130 mph ratings on their architectural lines, usually with conditions (specific starter strip, ridge cap, and nailing pattern). Read the conditions. A 130 mph rating with four nails and a generic starter is not a 130 mph roof. The reinforced nailing strips on Duration and HDZ are a real installation advantage because they make it harder for a crew to high-nail or miss the strip.

Impact Resistance

Each brand has a Class 4 product. The construction differs: some use a polymer-modified (SBS) asphalt that stays flexible and absorbs hail, others use a reinforced backing. In our experience, the SBS-modified products (Duration FLEX, Landmark ClimateFlex) handle Houston heat and hail very well. All qualify for Texas impact-resistant insurance discounts. See Class 4 impact-resistant shingles in Texas.

Algae Resistance

All three offer copper-granule algae-resistant versions. In humid, shaded Houston neighborhoods, this is worth specifying regardless of brand. The warranty length against algae streaking varies (10 to 25 years depending on product). See black streaks on a roof.

Warranties: Where the Real Difference Is

All three carry a limited lifetime material warranty on architectural shingles. The fine print: it covers manufacturing defects, it is prorated after an initial period, and it does not cover labor to tear off and replace unless you have an enhanced warranty. Enhanced warranties are where the brands differ and where the installer matters:

  • Owens Corning enhanced warranties (Preferred Protection and Platinum Protection) are available through Preferred and Platinum Preferred Contractors and add workmanship coverage and non-prorated periods.
  • GAF System Plus, Silver Pledge, and Golden Pledge warranties require certified or Master Elite contractors and a full GAF component system.
  • CertainTeed SureStart PLUS warranties through its credentialed contractors (ShingleMaster and SELECT ShingleMaster) similarly extend non-prorated coverage.

An enhanced warranty is only available if your roofer holds that brand's credential and installs the full system of that brand's components. If you care about warranty, pick the roofer's certification first and the brand second. See roof warranties explained.

Color and Look

Largely personal preference and HOA approval. Each brand has a similar range of browns, grays, and blacks. Owens Corning and CertainTeed offer a few more color-blended options in their premium lines. Get full-size samples, not the brochure, and look at them on the roof in sunlight. See popular roof shingle colors.

Price

At the same tier, the three are within a few percent of each other. Pricing differences on your estimate come from the tier (standard, Class 4, premium), the supplier's current pricing, and the roofer's margin, not from which logo is on the bundle. See how to read a roofing estimate.
