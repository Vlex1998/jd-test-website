Metal roofing has become more popular across Texas, and for good reason. It lasts a long time, sheds heat well, and holds up to strong wind. But it is not the right fit for every home or budget. Here is an honest look at metal roofing for Houston homeowners.

Types of Metal Roofing

  • Standing seam: panels joined with raised seams and concealed fasteners. It is the premium option, with a clean look, fewer leak points, and a long life. It also costs more.
  • Exposed fastener (screw-down) panels: panels screwed directly through the metal into the deck. They are more affordable and common on barns, shops, and some homes. The rubber washers on the screws age and need maintenance over time.
  • Metal shingles and tiles: stamped metal made to look like shingles, slate, or tile.

The Pros

  • Long lifespan. A well-installed metal roof often lasts much longer than asphalt shingles.
  • Wind resistance. Properly installed metal roofs perform well in high wind.
  • Heat reflection. Metal roofs with reflective coatings can reduce heat gain, which helps in Houston summers.
  • Low maintenance. No granule loss, and algae does not grow on metal the way it does on shingles.
  • Lightweight. Metal weighs less than tile and many shingle roofs.

The Cons

  • Higher upfront cost. Metal, especially standing seam, costs significantly more than asphalt shingles.
  • Hail dents. Large hail can dent metal panels. Dents are often cosmetic and do not cause leaks, but some insurance policies exclude cosmetic damage on metal roofs. Read your policy.
  • Noise. Rain can be louder on metal, though solid decking and underlayment reduce this a lot.
  • Specialized installation. Metal requires experienced installers. Mistakes at seams, flashing, and fasteners cause leaks.
  • Expansion and contraction. Metal moves with temperature changes, which installers have to account for.

What Affects the Cost

  • Panel type (standing seam vs exposed fastener)
  • Metal type and thickness (gauge)
  • Finish and coating quality
  • Roof size, pitch, and complexity
  • Tear-off of the existing roof

Is Metal Right for Your Home?

Metal makes the most sense if you plan to stay in your home for a long time and want a roof you will not have to think about. If you are selling in a few years, or if budget is the main concern, quality architectural or impact-resistant shingles may be a better value. Check your HOA rules too.
