Many neighborhoods across Houston, Katy, Cypress, Sugar Land, and The Woodlands have homeowners associations, and most HOAs have rules about roofing. Skipping the approval step can lead to fines, delays, or even being told to redo the work. Here is how to handle it.

What HOAs Typically Regulate

  • Shingle color, often limited to an approved list
  • Shingle style, such as requiring architectural or dimensional shingles instead of 3-tab
  • Materials, such as whether metal roofing is allowed
  • Work hours and where dumpsters can be placed
  • Approval timelines before work can begin

What Texas Law Says

Texas law limits how far HOAs can go on certain roofing products. Section 202.011 of the Texas Property Code generally prevents property owners' associations from banning shingles that are designed to resist wind and hail, provide heating and cooling efficiency, or generate solar energy, as long as the shingles resemble or are comparable to the shingles already used in the community and meet the association's other standards. In practice, this means your HOA usually cannot block impact-resistant shingles just because they are a different product. The details depend on your HOA's documents, so read them or ask the HOA if you are unsure.

How to Get HOA Approval

  1. Find your HOA's architectural guidelines. They are often on the HOA website or available from the management company.
  2. Choose a shingle and color that fits the guidelines. Your roofer can provide product samples and spec sheets.
  3. Submit the application. Most HOAs require a form with the product name, color, and the contractor's information.
  4. Wait for written approval before work begins. Approval times vary from a few days to several weeks.
  5. Keep a copy of the approval with your roofing paperwork.

Tips to Avoid Delays

  • Start the HOA process as soon as you sign with your roofer, or even before.
  • Include a photo or sample of the exact shingle color with your application.
  • Check whether neighbors have recently replaced roofs, and what they used.
  • For insurance claims, ask your roofer about storm-related emergency repairs, which may be handled differently.
