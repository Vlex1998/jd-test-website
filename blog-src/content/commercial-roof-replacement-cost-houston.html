Commercial roof pricing is quoted per square foot, and the range is wide because the roofs are so different: a 5,000 square foot strip center with a dozen HVAC units and a 100,000 square foot warehouse with a clean deck are not the same job. This guide covers what drives commercial roof replacement cost in Houston, rough ranges by system, and when a recover or restoration makes more sense than a full tear-off.

Rough Installed Ranges by System

For a typical low-slope commercial roof in the Houston area in 2026, installed costs commonly fall in these ranges. Small, cut-up roofs land at the top; large open roofs at the bottom.

  • TPO (single-ply, mechanically fastened or adhered): roughly $6 to $12 per square foot for a recover, more with tear-off and new insulation.
  • PVC single-ply: somewhat higher than TPO, often chosen for restaurants and roofs exposed to grease or chemicals.
  • Modified bitumen (two-ply): roughly $7 to $13 per square foot.
  • Silicone or acrylic coating over an existing roof: roughly $2 to $6 per square foot, depending on prep and thickness. See silicone vs acrylic coatings.
  • Standing seam metal (low-slope or sloped): roughly $12 to $20 per square foot.
  • Metal roof restoration (fasteners, seams, coating): a fraction of replacement.

Treat these as orientation, not a quote. A site inspection with core samples is the only way to price a specific building.

What Drives the Price

  • Tear-off vs recover. Removing the existing roof and wet insulation is a large share of the cost. Code allows a recover over one existing roof if it is dry and sound. Over two existing roofs, or over wet insulation, everything comes off.
  • Insulation. Current energy code requires a minimum insulation R-value on a full replacement. Adding polyiso insulation to meet code is a real line item on older buildings.
  • Deck condition. Rusted metal deck, rotted wood deck, or deteriorated lightweight concrete adds cost and sometimes structural work.
  • Penetrations and equipment. Every HVAC curb, pipe, drain, skylight, and vent takes flashing time. A restaurant roof with 30 penetrations costs far more per square foot than a warehouse with four.
  • Drainage. Adding tapered insulation to fix ponding, replacing drains, or adding scuppers.
  • Height and access. Crane or hoist rental for material to a multi-story roof, and staging in a tight parking lot.
  • Membrane thickness and attachment. 60-mil TPO costs more than 45-mil and lasts longer. Fully adhered costs more than mechanically fastened and performs better in wind.
  • Warranty. Manufacturer system warranties (15, 20, or 30 years with no dollar limit) have inspection and material requirements that add cost but add real protection.
  • Occupancy and schedule. Working around a retail tenant's hours or a school calendar costs more than an empty warehouse.

Recover, Restore, or Replace?

  • Coating or restoration makes sense when the existing roof is structurally sound, mostly dry, and the problems are surface-level: UV wear, minor seam failures, and ponding stains. It is the lowest-cost option and can add 10 to 20 years.
  • Recover (new membrane over the old roof with a separation layer or cover board) works when there is only one existing roof and the insulation is dry. It skips tear-off cost and disposal.
  • Full replacement is required when insulation is saturated, there are two layers already, or the deck needs work. It costs the most and resets the clock entirely.

Infrared moisture scans and core cuts tell you which one you need. Do not let anyone propose a coating over wet insulation; it traps the moisture and the roof fails from underneath. See signs your commercial roof needs replacement.

Houston-Specific Factors

  • Heat. White reflective membranes (TPO, PVC, or white coatings) measurably reduce cooling load on Houston buildings.
  • Hurricanes. Edge metal and attachment must meet wind uplift requirements, and in coastal counties, windstorm certification applies to commercial buildings too.
  • Rain. Drainage design matters more here than in dry climates. Ponding is the number one killer of Houston flat roofs. See commercial flat roof maintenance.

Comparing Bids

Bids should specify: membrane type and thickness, attachment method, insulation type and R-value, cover board, edge metal, flashing details at every penetration, drainage changes, warranty type and term, and whether tear-off and disposal are included. A low bid that omits insulation or uses 45-mil where the other bid has 60-mil is not the same roof. See TPO vs modified bitumen vs coatings for the system comparison.
