Houston is known for heat, not cold. But the February 2021 winter storm reminded everyone that hard freezes do happen here, and homes built for heat are not always ready for them. Here is how freezing weather can affect your roof, and what to watch for after a cold snap.

Ice on the Roof

In colder climates, "ice dams" form when snow melts on a warm roof and refreezes at the cold edge, backing water up under the shingles. True ice dams are uncommon in Houston, but freezing rain and sleet can still cause trouble:

  • Weight: heavy ice on gutters can bend or pull them off the fascia.
  • Water backup: as ice melts, water can pool behind frozen gutters and seep under the roof edge.
  • Brittle shingles: cold shingles are less flexible and more likely to crack from impacts or foot traffic.

Frozen Pipes in the Attic

Many Houston homes have water lines, water heaters, or HVAC condensate lines in the attic. When those freeze and burst, the damage looks like a roof leak, with water stains on the ceiling, but the roof is fine. If you see water damage after a freeze, check for plumbing leaks before you assume the roof failed. This matters for insurance too, since a burst pipe and a roof leak are handled differently.

Attic Condensation

During a cold snap, warm, humid air from the house can rise into a cold attic and condense on the underside of the roof decking. In poorly ventilated attics, you may see frost or water droplets on nails and decking, which then drip onto insulation. Over time, repeated condensation can lead to mold and rotten decking. Proper attic ventilation helps prevent it.

Thermal Shock

Big temperature swings, such as freezing nights followed by mild afternoons, make roofing materials expand and contract. This can loosen flashing, crack sealants around vents, and stress older shingles. It usually is not a single dramatic failure, but it speeds up wear.

What to Check After a Freeze

  1. Look at gutters for sagging or separation.
  2. Check ceilings and the attic for water stains or damp insulation.
  3. Inspect attic plumbing and HVAC lines for leaks.
  4. Look for frost or moisture on the underside of the decking on very cold mornings.
  5. From the ground, look for cracked or displaced shingles.

Preparing for the Next Freeze

  • Insulate attic water lines and know where your main water shutoff is.
  • Keep gutters clean so meltwater can drain.
  • Fix existing roof issues before winter so cold weather does not make them worse.
  • Make sure bathroom and kitchen exhaust fans vent outside, not into the attic.
