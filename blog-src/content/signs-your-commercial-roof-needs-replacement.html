Flat and low-slope commercial roofs do not fail all at once. They fail in stages, and the cost of each stage is much higher than the one before it. Catch a roof in the first stage and a coating or targeted repairs will carry it for years. Miss the signs and you are paying for saturated insulation, deck repairs, and interior damage on top of a new roof. Here are the eight signs that mean a Houston commercial roof is past patching.

1. Ponding Water That Does Not Drain Within 48 Hours

Water standing on a flat roof two days after rain is a design or deflection problem. Standing water accelerates membrane breakdown, grows algae, adds load, and finds every tiny defect. Occasional ponding can be fixed with added drains or tapered insulation. Widespread ponding with staining and soft areas means the insulation beneath is likely compressed or wet.

2. Open or Failing Seams

Single-ply roofs (TPO, PVC, EPDM) depend entirely on their seams. Lifting edges, fish-mouths, and seams that can be peeled by hand are the main entry point for water. A few failed seams can be re-welded. Seams failing across the whole roof mean the membrane has aged past repair.

3. Blisters, Bubbles, and Alligatoring

Blisters in modified bitumen or built-up roofs mean trapped moisture or gas under the surface. Alligatoring (a cracked pattern like dried mud) on asphalt or old coatings means the surface has lost its oils to UV. A handful of blisters can be cut and patched. Widespread blistering and alligatoring is a roof at the end of its life.

4. Saturated Insulation

You cannot see this from the surface, which is why it does the most damage. Wet polyiso or fiberboard insulation loses most of its R-value, adds enormous weight, rusts metal deck, and rots wood deck. Signs: soft or spongy areas underfoot, a roof that stays wet-looking in patches, high energy bills, and rust streaks at the deck inside. An infrared moisture scan or core cuts confirm it. Wet insulation cannot be coated or recovered; it has to come out.

5. Repair Costs Climbing Year Over Year

If you track repair spend, the trend tells the story. A roof that needed one repair three years ago, three last year, and six so far this year is not going to get better. When annual repairs reach 10 to 15 percent of a replacement cost, the math favors replacement. See commercial roof replacement cost in Houston.

6. Interior Leaks in More Than One Area

One leak is a repair. Leaks in multiple areas of the building, or leaks that move after repairs, mean water is traveling under the membrane and the system is compromised. Stained ceiling tiles, wet insulation above the ceiling, and rust on deck flutes are all signs.

7. Flashing and Edge Failures

Flashing at HVAC curbs, parapet walls, drains, and the roof edge is where most commercial leaks start. Separated base flashing, cracked pitch pockets, rusted edge metal, and counterflashing pulling away from walls are all repairable individually. When they fail everywhere at once, the roof is telling you its age.

8. Age and Warranty Status

Typical Houston service lives: TPO 15 to 25 years depending on thickness, modified bitumen 15 to 25, EPDM 20 to 30, built-up 20 to 30, metal 40 plus with maintenance. A roof past its warranty and near the end of its typical life should be inspected annually and budgeted for replacement, even if it is not leaking yet.

What a Commercial Roof Assessment Should Include

  • Visual inspection of membrane, seams, flashings, drains, and edges with photos
  • Infrared moisture survey or core samples to find wet insulation
  • Deck inspection from below where possible
  • A map of problem areas
  • Options with costs: repair, coat, recover, or replace
  • An expected remaining life for each option

Options Short of Full Replacement

If insulation is dry and the deck is sound, a silicone or acrylic coating can add a decade to a tired membrane, and a recover (new membrane over the old) avoids tear-off cost.
