Mature trees are one of the best things about many Houston neighborhoods. They provide shade, privacy, and curb appeal. But trees close to your roof bring real risks. Here is what to watch for and how to keep both your trees and your roof healthy.

How Trees Damage Roofs

  • Falling limbs. In storms, a large limb can puncture shingles, crack decking, or damage rafters. Dead branches are the biggest risk.
  • Branch abrasion. Branches that touch the roof scrape away granules every time the wind blows, wearing down shingles.
  • Leaves and needles. Debris collects in valleys and gutters, holding moisture and blocking drainage.
  • Shade and moisture. Shaded roofs stay damp longer, which encourages algae, moss, and rot. See black streaks on roofs.
  • Pests. Overhanging branches give squirrels, raccoons, and rats an easy path to your roof and attic.

Signs Your Trees Are a Problem

  • Branches touching or hanging directly over the roof
  • Piles of leaves or needles in valleys
  • Moss or heavy algae on shaded slopes
  • Scrape marks or worn spots on shingles
  • Animal activity in the attic
  • Dead or cracked limbs above the house

How to Manage Trees Near Your Roof

  • Keep branches trimmed back from the roof. A common recommendation is several feet of clearance, but an arborist can give advice for your specific trees.
  • Remove dead limbs before hurricane season.
  • Clean gutters and valleys more often if you have heavy tree cover.
  • Hire a professional for large limbs. Cutting branches over a roof is dangerous and can cause the very damage you are trying to avoid.

When a Tree Falls on Your Roof

  1. Get everyone out of the affected area.
  2. Watch for downed power lines and call the utility if needed.
  3. Photograph the damage before anything is moved.
  4. Call a tree service to remove the tree and a roofer to tarp the opening.
  5. Contact your insurance company. Damage from a tree falling on a house during a storm is commonly covered, but check your policy.
