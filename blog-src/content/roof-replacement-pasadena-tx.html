Pasadena and Deer Park are two of the oldest suburbs on the southeast side of Houston. Much of the housing went up between the 1950s and 1980s to serve the Ship Channel and the plants along Highway 225. That age, combined with hurricane exposure and a lot of original low-slope construction, makes roofing here different from a 2010 subdivision in Cypress.

What We See on Pasadena Roofs

  • Multiple shingle layers. It was common for decades to roof over the old layer instead of tearing off. We regularly find two layers, and sometimes a third hiding under the top two. All of it has to come off.
  • Plank decking. Pre-1970s homes often have 1x6 or 1x8 boards instead of plywood. Gaps between boards and rotted ends at the eaves are common, and modern shingles need solid support.
  • Low-slope additions. Enclosed patios, carports, and rear additions with 1:12 or 2:12 pitch that were shingled anyway and have leaked ever since.
  • Old flashing. Chimneys, wall intersections, and vents with flashing that was caulked over several times instead of replaced. See chimney flashing leaks.
  • No underlayment, or disintegrated felt. Older roofs sometimes have nothing but shingles between the weather and the deck.

Hurricanes and Wind

Pasadena is roughly twenty miles from the Gulf and close enough to the bay that hurricane wind is the primary storm risk, more than hail. Ike, Harvey, and Beryl all left their marks here. The west side of the city is outside the TWIA windstorm zone, but the far east edge of Pasadena and parts of Deer Park and La Porte along Highway 146 fall inside it. If you are in that strip, see our guide to windstorm certification.

Regardless of zone, a Pasadena replacement should be installed to a high-wind standard: six nails per shingle, starter strip at rakes and eaves, sealed deck, and metal edge. Wind damage signs explains why edges and ridges go first.

Low-Slope Sections Need a Different Roof

Shingles are designed to shed water on a slope. Below about 2:12, water does not run off fast enough and works under the shingles. The right answer for those porch and addition roofs is a membrane: modified bitumen (torch-down or self-adhered) or TPO. It costs a bit more per square foot but stops the chronic leaks. Our commercial membrane guide covers the same products used on houses.

Heat and Ventilation

A lot of older Pasadena homes have little or no attic ventilation, which cooks shingles from below and pushes up cooling bills. A re-roof is the best time to add ridge vent or more intake at the soffits. Read roof ventilation and attic heat.

Permits

The City of Pasadena and the City of Deer Park both require permits for roof replacement, and the roofer should be registered with the city. If a contractor suggests skipping the permit, that is a warning sign. See roof permits in the Houston area.

Because of the layer removal, decking, and flashing work, an older Pasadena home often costs more to re-roof than a same-sized newer home in a subdivision.
