Humble, Kingwood, and Atascocita share Lake Houston, the San Jacinto River, and a lot of pine and oak canopy. That tree cover is a big reason people move here, and it is also the reason roofs in this part of Harris County have their own set of problems. Our shop on Veterans Memorial is a short drive west, so we spend a lot of time on roofs in the 77338, 77339, 77345, and 77346 zip codes. Here is what we see.

Trees Are the Number One Issue

Kingwood is nicknamed the Livable Forest for a reason. Mature pines drop needles year-round, and needles pack into valleys, behind chimneys, and in gutters. Wet needle mats hold moisture against shingles and feed algae and moss. Overhanging limbs scrape granules off in the wind, and in a strong storm they come down on the roof. Read overhanging trees and your roof for how to manage it.

Heavy shade also means slow drying. Roofs under canopy in Kingwood show the black algae streaks and lichen growth much earlier than roofs in open subdivisions.

Flooding Changed the Housing Stock

Harvey in 2017 flooded large parts of Kingwood and the Forest Cove and Elm Grove areas, and the 2019 Imelda rains hit again. A lot of homes were gutted and rebuilt inside, but roofs were often left alone. If you bought a renovated home here, do not assume the roof was part of the renovation. A roof inspection before closing is worth it.

Neighborhood by Neighborhood

  • Kingwood villages (Trailwood, Bear Branch, Kings Point, Hunters Ridge, Elm Grove). Mostly 1970s to 1990s homes. Many are on their second or third roof. Watch for multiple layers and old plywood decking. Strong HOAs with color guidelines.
  • Atascocita (Eagle Springs, Atascocita Shores, Walden on Lake Houston). Mix of 1980s lakefront homes and 2000s subdivisions. Newer homes are reaching the 15-year mark where builder shingles give out.
  • Humble proper and Fall Creek. Older homes near downtown Humble often have low-slope sections and additions that need careful flashing. Fall Creek and Summerwood have HOA requirements similar to Kingwood.
  • Porter and New Caney. Just north in Montgomery County. More acreage, more metal roofs, fewer HOA constraints.

Storm Exposure

This side of Houston gets the same spring hail as the rest of the metro and sits in the path of storms moving up from the Gulf. The 2024 derecho and Hurricane Beryl both did heavy damage in Kingwood and Humble, mostly from wind and fallen trees. If you have storm damage, start with what to do right after a storm and our Texas roof insurance claim guide.

What We Recommend for Lake Houston Homes

  • Algae-resistant architectural shingles. Most major brands offer copper-granule lines that stay cleaner under shade.
  • Gutter guards or at least a twice-a-year cleaning, because pine needles will fill open gutters in a season. See gutters and roof drainage.
  • Ice and water shield in every valley and around chimneys, where debris holds water the longest.
  • Trim limbs back at least six to ten feet from the roof surface before the new roof goes on.
  • Metal is worth a look on acreage lots and for homeowners who want to stop thinking about the roof. See metal roofing in Texas.

Permits and HOA

The City of Humble requires a permit for re-roofing inside its limits. Kingwood and Atascocita are mostly inside the City of Houston (Kingwood was annexed in 1996), so a City of Houston permit applies. Most Kingwood villages route roofing through the Kingwood Service Association or village-level architectural control. Allow a couple of weeks for approval.
