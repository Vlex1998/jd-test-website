Tomball and Magnolia are where the Houston suburbs turn into pine country. Lots get bigger, trees get taller, and you start to see as many metal roofs as shingle roofs. We are based just southeast on Veterans Memorial, so this is home territory for us. Here is what matters for roofs in northwest Harris County and southern Montgomery County.

Two Different Housing Types

  • Subdivision homes in places like Woodtrace, Wildwood at Northpointe, Rosehill Reserve, Amira, and the Magnolia side of 249. Mostly 2005 to present, architectural shingles, HOA rules, and tight lots. These roofs are now reaching the age where original builder shingles are failing.
  • Acreage and ranch properties along FM 2920, FM 1488, and FM 1774. Older homes, barns, workshops, and a high share of metal. Fewer HOA constraints and more freedom in material choice.

Hail and Spring Storms

The 249 corridor from Tomball up through Pinehurst and Magnolia gets hit by hail regularly. Supercells that build to the northwest tend to track right across this area in March through May. If you have been through a hail event, read how to spot hail damage and how many hail hits it takes to replace a roof. Upgrading to Class 4 shingles makes more sense here than almost anywhere in the metro.

Pine Trees

Tall loblolly pines drop needles continuously and shed limbs in wind. On a shingle roof, needles pack into valleys and behind any obstruction, hold water, and grow moss. On a metal roof they slide off, which is one reason metal is so popular on wooded lots. Either way, keep limbs trimmed back and valleys clear. See overhanging trees and your roof.

Metal Roofing Out Here

Metal makes a strong case in Tomball and Magnolia: it sheds pine debris, handles hail better than standard shingles, lasts two to three times as long, and reflects heat. The questions are cost, panel type, and whether your HOA allows it. Standing seam is the premium option. Exposed-fastener panels are cheaper but need screw maintenance over time. Read metal roofing in Texas: pros, cons, and cost before you decide.

Workshops, Barns, and Outbuildings

Acreage properties usually have at least one outbuilding with a roof that has not been looked at in years. Rusted screws, backed-out fasteners, and failed sealant at laps are the typical problems on older metal. Many of these can be restored with new fasteners and a coating rather than replaced. Ask about this when you have the house roof inspected.

Permits and HOA

The City of Tomball and the City of Magnolia require permits inside their limits. Most of the surrounding area is unincorporated Harris or Montgomery County, where no residential re-roof permit is required. Subdivision HOAs usually require architectural approval for color and material, and some restrict metal to standing seam in specific colors. See HOA rules and roof replacement in Texas.
