It's a fair question: does a roofing company need to have been around for decades to do good work? The honest answer is that company age matters, but it's not the whole picture.

What Company Age Tells You

  • Survivability: roofing is a tough, competitive business. A company that has operated for 10+ years has weathered slow seasons, material price swings, and insurance disputes and is still standing.
  • A visible track record: older companies usually have a longer history of reviews, completed projects, and local references you can actually verify.
  • Established manufacturer relationships: certifications like GAF Certified Contractor or Owens Corning Preferred Contractor typically take time and a proven installation record to earn and maintain.

What It Doesn't Tell You

Age alone isn't a guarantee of quality, and a newer company isn't automatically a risk. What matters more than the number of years in business is whether the company is properly insured, uses certified installers, stands behind its work with a real workmanship warranty, and has a track record you can verify through reviews and references — regardless of how long the name has been on a truck.

What to Check Instead

  • Current general liability and workers' compensation insurance
  • Manufacturer certifications (GAF, CertainTeed, Owens Corning, etc.)
  • A written workmanship warranty, not just the manufacturer's material warranty
  • Recent, verifiable Google reviews from local customers
