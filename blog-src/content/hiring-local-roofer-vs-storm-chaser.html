After a major hailstorm or hurricane moves through Houston, two kinds of roofers show up: local companies that have served the area for years, and out-of-town "storm chasers" who follow bad weather from state to state looking for quick work. Knowing the difference can save you from a botched repair and a contractor who's impossible to reach once your roof starts leaking again.

What Is a Storm Chaser?

A storm chaser is a crew that travels to whichever region just got hit by a major storm, canvasses neighborhoods door-to-door, and moves on once the local demand dries up. They often operate under a company name that isn't registered locally, use out-of-state license plates, and may not carry the insurance or manufacturer certifications they claim to.

Why Local Matters

  • Accountability: a local company has a physical address, a track record in the community, and a reputation to protect long after the job is done.
  • Warranty service: if a shingle lifts or a flashing leaks two years from now, a local roofer is still around to fix it. A storm chaser may already be three states away.
  • Permits and code knowledge: local contractors know Houston and Harris County permitting requirements and inspect to the standards local building departments expect.
  • Insurance claim experience: a company that works with the same local adjusters year-round tends to navigate the claims process more smoothly.

Red Flags to Watch For

  • Door-to-door solicitation immediately after a storm, before you've called anyone
  • Pressure to sign a contract the same day
  • Requests for full payment upfront, before any work begins
  • No local address or a P.O. box instead of a real office
  • Vague or verbal-only estimates with nothing in writing
