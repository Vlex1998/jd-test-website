Unlike a lot of home service trades, residential roofing usually isn't billed by the hour. Most roof replacements and many repairs are priced per "square" (100 square feet of roof) or as a flat project bid based on materials, labor, and roof complexity, not a per-hour labor rate.

Why Roofing Isn't Typically Hourly

A roofing crew's speed depends heavily on roof pitch, complexity, weather, and material type, so an hourly rate wouldn't give you a reliable way to compare quotes or predict your final cost. Instead, reputable contractors price the whole job upfront based on the scope of work, so you know the total cost before anything starts.

What Actually Drives the Price

  • Roof size (measured in squares)
  • Material type (3-tab, architectural, designer shingles, metal, tile, etc.)
  • Roof pitch and complexity (number of valleys, dormers, chimneys)
  • Tear-off and disposal of the old roof
  • Any decking repair or replacement needed
  • Local permit requirements

When Hourly Rates Do Show Up

Small, standalone repair jobs (like resealing a single vent boot) are sometimes billed as a flat service call fee rather than hourly, but it's still uncommon for residential roofers to quote a strict per-hour rate the way a plumber or electrician might.
