Choosing a roofing material means balancing upfront cost, expected lifespan, aesthetics, and how well it holds up to Houston's climate. Here's an overview of the main categories.

Asphalt Shingles

The most common residential roofing material in the U.S., available in three tiers: 3-tab (budget, shorter lifespan), architectural/dimensional (the most popular middle tier), and designer/premium (top-tier durability and looks). Read our full shingle guide for a detailed breakdown.

Metal Roofing

Known for durability (often 40+ years), excellent heat reflectivity, and strong wind resistance — a good fit for Texas heat, though the upfront cost is higher than asphalt shingles.

Tile Roofing

Clay or concrete tile offers a distinctive look common on Spanish and Mediterranean-style homes, with an extremely long material lifespan. It's heavier than shingles, so roof structure needs to support the added weight.

Slate Roofing

A premium natural stone option known for exceptional longevity and a high-end appearance, though it's among the most expensive materials and requires specialized installation.

Wood Shake

A natural, textured look that many designer asphalt shingles are actually designed to mimic. True wood shake requires more maintenance and carries fire-rating considerations worth discussing with your insurer.

Flat and Low-Slope Roofing (Commercial & Some Residential)

TPO, EPDM, and modified bitumen systems are common on flat or low-slope roofs, typically seen on commercial buildings and additions. Learn more on our commercial roofing page.
