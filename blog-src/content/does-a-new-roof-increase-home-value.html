A new roof adds value to a home, but usually not dollar for dollar. Where it makes the biggest difference is not the appraisal number. It is whether the house sells at all, how fast, and whether the buyer's inspection turns into a renegotiation. Here is how roofs factor into Houston home sales from the buyer, appraiser, lender, and inspector's point of view.

How Much Value Does a New Roof Add?

National remodeling studies have consistently put the resale value of an asphalt roof replacement at roughly 55 to 70 percent of its cost. In other words, a replacement that costs $18,000 might add $10,000 to $12,000 to the sale price. That looks like a loss until you consider the alternative: selling with an old roof.

What an Old Roof Costs You at Sale

  • Inspection repair requests. Houston buyers almost always get a general inspection, and the roof is the first thing on the report. A 15-plus-year-old roof with any defects becomes a repair demand or a credit request, often for the full replacement cost, not 60 percent of it.
  • Insurance problems for the buyer. Many Texas carriers will not write a new policy on a roof over 15 to 20 years old, or will only offer actual cash value coverage. If the buyer cannot get insurance, they cannot close on a mortgage. See does a new roof lower insurance.
  • Lender and appraisal conditions. FHA and VA appraisers flag roofs with visible damage or less than a couple years of remaining life, and can require repair before closing.
  • Lower offers and longer days on market. Buyers mentally subtract a new roof from their offer, and usually round up.
  • Seller's disclosure. Texas requires you to disclose known roof defects and leaks. A known leak you did not fix is a disclosure item that affects every offer.

Put together, an old roof commonly costs a seller more in concessions and lost offers than a replacement would have cost outright.

What Buyers Actually Respond To

A new roof in a listing signals that the house has been maintained. It removes the single largest unknown expense from the buyer's mental math. Agents in Houston will tell you a listing that says "new roof 2026" gets more showings than one that says nothing about the roof, especially on homes built in the 1990s and 2000s where buyers assume the roof is original.

Transferable Warranties Add Value

Most manufacturer warranties can be transferred once to a new owner within a set window after the roof is installed, and some workmanship warranties transfer too. Keep the paperwork and make sure the listing mentions it. See roof warranties explained.

Should You Replace Before Selling?

Replace if:

  • The roof is leaking, visibly damaged, or near the end of its life
  • Your agent says it will be an inspection or insurance problem
  • You want a clean, fast sale without renegotiation

Consider a credit instead if:

  • The roof is older but sound and insurable, and the market is strong
  • You do not want to front the cash and would rather negotiate

A middle path is to get a written roof inspection and a replacement quote before listing, so you can respond to a buyer's request with facts. See what to expect during a roof inspection.

Choosing a Roof for Resale

If the goal is resale value, spend on the things buyers and inspectors notice: architectural shingles in a neutral color, clean flashing and ridge, new vents, and a transferable warranty. Impact-resistant shingles are worth mentioning in the listing because they lower the buyer's insurance. Expensive upgrades like premium designer shingles rarely pay back at sale.
