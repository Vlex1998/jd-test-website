Baytown sits where the San Jacinto River meets Galveston Bay, surrounded by refineries, chemical plants, and open water. It is a working town with a wide range of housing, from 1950s homes near Old Baytown to new subdivisions off Highway 146 and I-10. Roofing here has a few extra considerations that homeowners in north or west Houston never think about.

Windstorm Zone

Baytown straddles the line. Parts of the city east of State Highway 146 fall inside the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association catastrophe area, and so does everything in Chambers County, including the Baytown subdivisions that spill across the county line. Homes there typically carry wind and hail coverage through TWIA and need a WPI-8 certificate on any new roof to stay insurable.

That means a windstorm-code installation with an inspection during the job. Our League City and Clear Lake guide goes into the WPI-8 process in detail. Homes west of 146 in Baytown are generally outside the zone, but confirm by address.

Industrial Air and Roof Life

Baytown's plants are well regulated, but there is more airborne particulate and sulfur in the air here than in a suburb thirty miles inland. On roofs, that shows up as faster weathering of bare metal, more frequent staining, and shingles that look older than they are. It is not a reason to avoid any material, but it is a reason to choose coated flashing and fasteners and to rinse roofs and gutters occasionally.

Hurricane Exposure

Ike came ashore just south of Baytown in 2008 and did enormous roof damage. Beryl in 2024 and the storms since have reminded everyone that this is a coastal town. When your roof is replaced, build for the next one:

  • High-wind shingle installation with six nails per shingle
  • Sealed deck seams and full synthetic underlayment
  • Ice and water shield at eaves, valleys, and around all penetrations
  • Metal drip edge fastened on the tight spacing windstorm code calls for
  • Hurricane straps or clips on older homes if the attic framing is accessible during the job

Our hurricane season checklist is worth reading every spring.

Older Homes and Multiple Layers

Many Baytown homes built in the 1950s through 1970s have had two or even three layers of shingles stacked over the years. Code only allows two, and a second layer adds weight, traps heat, and hides decking problems. When these homes are re-roofed, everything comes off down to the deck, and it is common to replace a fair amount of plank or plywood decking. Read can you put new shingles over old ones and hidden costs of roof replacement so there are no surprises.

Low-Slope and Flat Sections

A lot of mid-century Baytown homes have low-slope porch roofs, carports, or additions. Shingles are not rated for anything under 2:12 pitch. Those sections should get a modified bitumen or TPO membrane rather than more shingles. See TPO vs modified bitumen vs coatings.

Permits

The City of Baytown requires a building permit for roof replacement. Your contractor should be registered with the city and pull the permit in their name, not yours. Mont Belvieu and Chambers County have their own rules, and the windstorm inspection adds a step on top of the city permit.

Costs

Baytown replacement pricing runs in line with the rest of the Houston area for standard installs. Windstorm-certified installations cost somewhat more because of the inspection fee and extra fastening and sealing. See how much a roof costs in Houston and roof financing options.
