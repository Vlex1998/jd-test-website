Shingle color affects your home's curb appeal, resale value, and even attic temperature. Here are some of the most widely used, versatile color families available from the manufacturers we install — GAF, CertainTeed, and Owens Corning.

Popular Color Families

  • Weathered Wood: a warm, blended brown that pairs well with most brick and siding colors.
  • Charcoal: a deep, near-black gray that gives a bold, modern look.
  • Driftwood: a soft gray-brown blend, one of the most versatile neutral options.
  • Slate Gray: a cool, blue-toned gray that suits contemporary and traditional homes alike.
  • Pewter Gray: a lighter, silvery gray with subtle dimension.
  • Barkwood: a rich brown with warm undertones, popular on craftsman-style homes.
  • Hickory: a lighter tan-brown blend that works well with earth-tone exteriors.
  • Shakewood: designed to mimic the look of natural wood shake in a dimensional shingle.
  • Estate Gray: a balanced medium gray, one of the most common "safe choice" colors.
  • Williamsburg Slate: a deep blue-gray blend with a classic, historic feel.
  • Black Oak: a nearly black shade with brown undertones for high contrast.
  • Moire Black: a true, uniform black for a sharp, high-contrast roofline.

Choosing the Right Color for Your Home

  • Lighter colors reflect more heat, which can help with cooling costs in Houston's climate
  • Consider your brick, siding, and trim colors together, not the shingle in isolation
  • Check any HOA restrictions on approved roof colors before deciding
  • Ask to see a physical sample on your own roof style, since color can look different in full sun versus a small swatch
