Missouri City and Stafford cover a wide range of Fort Bend County housing, from 1970s neighborhoods along Highway 6 and Murphy Road to brand new sections of Sienna. The roofing needs are just as varied. This guide walks through what homeowners in southwest Houston should know, neighborhood by neighborhood.

Older Missouri City and Stafford

Quail Valley, Hunters Glen, Fondren Park, and the Stafford neighborhoods near Cash Road and Fifth Street were largely built in the 1970s and 1980s. Common issues on these roofs:

  • Second or third shingle layers from decades of roof-overs
  • Original plywood decking that has delaminated from heat and attic moisture
  • Wood shake or shingle roofs on some 1970s homes that were covered over, which need a full tear-off and often new decking
  • Poor attic ventilation, which shortens shingle life. See roof ventilation and attic heat.

Budget for decking replacement and ask for a per-sheet price in writing. Hidden costs of roof replacement covers what to expect.

Sienna, Riverstone, and the Newer Communities

Sienna, Riverstone, Lake Olympia, and Vicksburg are master-planned communities with active HOAs and architectural review. Most homes were built between 2000 and today with architectural shingles. The first wave of Sienna homes is now hitting the 20-year mark, and the 2008 to 2014 sections are reaching the age where builder-grade shingles start losing granules.

HOA rules here usually specify shingle style and an approved color list, and require written approval before work. Texas law requires HOAs to allow impact-resistant and energy-efficient shingles that resemble the approved products. See HOA rules and roof replacement in Texas.

Storm Exposure

Fort Bend County gets hail most springs and sits in the path of tropical systems moving inland from the coast. Missouri City had widespread wind damage from Beryl in 2024. After any major storm, check your roof from the ground and read what to do right after a storm. Be cautious with door-knockers; local roofer vs storm chaser explains the warning signs.

Drainage and Heavy Rain

This area is flat and drains slowly. Gutters that overflow dump water against the foundation, which in Fort Bend's clay soil means foundation movement. Proper gutters and downspout extensions are a roof issue and a foundation issue. See gutters and roof drainage.

Permits

Both the City of Missouri City and the City of Stafford require permits for roof replacement. Some neighborhoods with a Missouri City address are actually in unincorporated Fort Bend County, which has no residential re-roof permit. Your roofer should check the address. See roof permits in the Houston area.
