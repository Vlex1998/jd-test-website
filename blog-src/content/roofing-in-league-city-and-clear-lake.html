League City, Clear Lake, Webster, Seabrook, and Kemah sit on the bay side of the Houston area. Roofs here deal with things most of the metro does not: salt air, higher sustained wind, storm surge zones, and the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association. If you are repairing or replacing a roof in Galveston County or the coastal edge of Harris County, this guide covers what is different.

TWIA and the WPI-8 Certificate

Most of Galveston County, including League City, Friendswood's Galveston County side, Dickinson, Kemah, and Seabrook, is in the designated catastrophe area where many homeowners carry wind and hail coverage through TWIA rather than their regular insurer. Harris County homes east of State Highway 146 are in the same zone.

To be insurable through TWIA, roof work on these homes must be inspected and certified to meet windstorm building code. The result is a WPI-8 certificate of compliance, issued after a Texas Department of Insurance appointed inspector or a licensed engineer verifies the installation. Without it, you may not be able to get or keep TWIA coverage on the home. Key points:

  • The inspection has to happen during the job, before the shingles cover the decking and underlayment. It cannot be done after the fact.
  • Your roofer needs to schedule it. Ask up front whether they handle WPI-8 inspections and who their engineer is.
  • Windstorm code details differ from a standard install: specific nailing patterns, decking attachment, sealed roof deck requirements, and approved products.

If you are in a Clear Lake neighborhood west of 146 (most of Clear Lake City, parts of Webster), you are usually outside the TWIA zone and a standard permit applies. Your roofer should confirm this by address before quoting.

Wind Is the Design Driver

This area routinely sees stronger sustained winds than north Houston, and in a hurricane it is closer to landfall. A roof here should be built for wind first:

  • Shingles with a 130 mph rating installed with six nails and the manufacturer's high-wind pattern
  • Starter strips at eaves and rakes, not cut-up field shingles
  • Properly fastened drip edge and metal edge details
  • Sealed or taped deck seams under the underlayment, which keeps water out even if shingles blow off

Salt Air and Metal

Within a few miles of the bay, salt air shortens the life of unprotected metal. That matters for flashing, vents, and fasteners more than for shingles. Ask for galvanized or painted flashing, stainless or coated fasteners near the water, and if you are considering a metal roof, look at Galvalume or aluminum rather than bare galvanized panels. Insurance Claims Near the Coast
