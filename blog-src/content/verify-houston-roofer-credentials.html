Unlike states such as Florida or Louisiana, Texas does not require roofing contractors to hold a state-issued roofing license. That surprises a lot of homeowners, and it means the burden of vetting a roofer falls more heavily on you. Here's what to actually check.

Texas Doesn't License Roofers Statewide

There's no Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation license specific to roofing contractors, so "licensed" isn't the credential to ask about here the way it might be in other trades or other states. Some cities and counties have their own registration or permitting requirements, so it's worth asking whether a contractor pulls permits and is registered to work in your specific municipality.

What to Check Instead

  • General liability and workers' comp insurance: ask for a current certificate of insurance, and call the carrier to confirm it's active if you want to be thorough.
  • Manufacturer certifications: credentials like GAF Certified Contractor, CertainTeed Master Shingle Applicator, or Owens Corning Preferred Contractor mean a manufacturer has vetted the company's installation practices.
  • Better Business Bureau standing and Google reviews: look for a consistent pattern of real, recent reviews, not just a handful from years ago.
  • A real local address: a company you can visit, not just a phone number.
  • A written contract and workmanship warranty: get everything in writing before any work or payment happens.
