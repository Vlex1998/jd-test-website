Whether you need a permit to replace a roof depends entirely on which jurisdiction your house is in, and in the Houston area that is more complicated than it sounds. A Houston mailing address does not mean you are in the City of Houston. Here is how it breaks down and what it means for your project.

City of Houston: Yes

Inside Houston city limits, a building permit is required to replace a roof covering. The contractor applies through the city's permitting system, pays the fee, and the work is subject to inspection. This applies to Kingwood, Clear Lake City, and other annexed areas, not just the inner loop. The permit cost is modest and is usually built into the roofer's price.

Unincorporated Harris County: Generally No

Harris County does not have a residential building code enforcement program for re-roofing. If your home is in an unincorporated area, which includes large parts of what people call Katy, Cypress, Spring, Humble, Atascocita, and the FM 1960 corridor, there is no county permit to pull for shingle replacement. Exceptions: homes in a floodplain may need a floodplain development permit for structural changes, and homes in a municipal utility district or HOA may have their own approval requirements.

Surrounding Cities: Mostly Yes

Most incorporated suburbs require a re-roof permit and require the contractor to be registered with the city. That includes:

  • Pearland, Friendswood, League City, Webster, Seabrook
  • Sugar Land, Missouri City, Stafford, Rosenberg, Richmond
  • Katy (city proper), Fulshear
  • Pasadena, Deer Park, La Porte, Baytown
  • Humble, Tomball, Jersey Village, Conroe
  • Bellaire, West University Place, Southside Place, and the other inner-loop cities

The Woodlands is a special case: it is not a city, but the township and the covenant administration review roofing for compliance with community standards, and Montgomery County has no re-roof permit. Fort Bend and Brazoria counties also do not require permits in unincorporated areas, but their cities do.

Coastal Windstorm Zone: Extra Step

Galveston County, Chambers County, and Harris County east of Highway 146 are in the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association catastrophe area. There, a roof needs a WPI-8 windstorm certificate to keep TWIA coverage, which requires an inspection during installation. This is separate from and in addition to any city permit. Who Pulls the Permit

The contractor should. When a roofer pulls a permit, they are registered with that city, on record as the responsible party, and subject to the inspection. When a roofer asks you to pull it as a homeowner, they are either not registered with the city or do not want their name attached. Either way, it is a reason to ask more questions. See questions to ask a roofer before hiring.

What the Inspection Checks

City inspections on a re-roof are usually brief and happen after completion. The inspector confirms the permit, checks that drip edge, underlayment, and flashing meet code, checks the number of shingle layers, and looks at vent and penetration details. Some cities do an in-progress inspection of the decking before it is covered. The windstorm inspection is more involved and must happen before shingles cover the deck.

What Happens If You Skip It

  • The city can issue a stop-work order and fines, and require you to open up finished work for inspection.
  • Unpermitted work can surface in a title search or buyer inspection when you sell and become a negotiating problem.
  • Some insurers ask for permit records when a roof is replaced and when a claim is filed.

Repairs vs Replacement

Most cities do not require a permit for minor repairs, usually defined as under one square (100 square feet) or a small percentage of the roof. Full replacement always needs one where permits apply. If you are replacing decking or changing the structure, a permit is typically required even in a repair.

HOA Approval Is Separate

A permit is a government requirement. HOA architectural approval is a private contractual one. You may need both, neither, or one. See HOA rules and roof replacement in Texas.
