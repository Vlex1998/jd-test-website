For most Texas homeowners, yes, a new roof lowers the premium, and in some cases it is the difference between being able to buy a policy at all and being turned down. Roof age has become one of the biggest rating factors in Texas home insurance, and insurers have gotten much stricter about it since the run of hail and hurricane years. Here is how it works and how to make sure you get every discount you qualify for.

Why Insurers Care So Much About Roof Age

Roof claims are the largest single category of homeowners losses in Texas. An older roof is more likely to leak, more likely to lose shingles in wind, and more likely to be a total loss after hail. Carriers have responded in a few ways:

  • Refusing to write new policies on homes with roofs over a certain age, commonly 15 to 20 years for asphalt shingles.
  • Switching older roofs to actual cash value coverage, so a claim pays only the depreciated value instead of replacement cost. See ACV vs RCV.
  • Raising wind and hail deductibles to 2 percent or more of the dwelling value.
  • Requiring a roof inspection before renewing a policy on a home with a 10-plus-year-old roof.

A new roof reverses all of that. You become a lower-risk home, and the premium reflects it.

Discounts to Ask For

  • New roof discount. Most carriers tier the premium by roof age. A roof under five years old gets the best rate. Some apply it automatically when you report the replacement; others need you to ask.
  • Impact-resistant roof discount. Texas insurers are required to offer a premium reduction for roofs that meet UL 2218 Class 3 or Class 4 impact standards. The discount on the wind and hail portion of the premium can be meaningful. You will need the shingle product name and often a certificate from the roofer. See Class 4 impact-resistant shingles.
  • Roof material discount. Metal and tile roofs sometimes rate better than asphalt for wind and fire.
  • Windstorm mitigation credits. In coastal counties and the TWIA zone, a roof installed to windstorm code with a WPI-8 certificate can lower the wind premium. See our coastal roofing guide.
  • Wind-rated shingles and sealed deck. A few carriers credit roofs with documented high-wind installation.

Paperwork Your Insurer Will Want

  • The roofer's invoice showing the completion date and the full scope of work
  • The shingle manufacturer and product name, and its impact rating if applicable
  • Photos of the finished roof (your roofer should provide these)
  • For impact-resistant discounts, the carrier's own form, sometimes signed by the contractor
  • The WPI-8 certificate if the home is in the windstorm zone

Send this to your agent as soon as the job is done. The discount usually starts at the next renewal, and some carriers will re-rate mid-term.

How Much Can You Save?

It depends on the carrier, the home, and the roof. Moving from a 20-year-old 3-tab roof to a new Class 4 architectural roof commonly reduces the premium by a noticeable percentage, and more importantly restores replacement cost coverage on the roof. On a home where the carrier was about to non-renew because of roof age, the savings is the whole policy. Get quotes from your current carrier and at least one other after the roof is done. New-roof pricing differs a lot between companies.

Does Replacing the Roof After a Claim Raise Rates?

A paid claim can affect your rate for a few years, but the new roof partly offsets it. Insurers look at the whole picture: claim history, roof age, and construction. One storm claim followed by a new roof is a normal file in Texas and is not a red flag.

Plan the Replacement With Insurance in Mind

If your roof is 15-plus years old and you are getting non-renewal warnings or inspection requests, replacing it on your own terms, before a storm forces the issue, keeps you in control of the product, the contractor, and the timing. Financing is available, and the premium savings offset part of the payment.
