The roof is the most expensive single component a home buyer can get wrong. A general home inspector spends a few minutes on it, usually from a ladder or the ground, and writes something like "roof appears to be in serviceable condition, recommend evaluation by a licensed roofing contractor." That sentence is doing a lot of work. Here is why a separate roof inspection is worth it before you close on a Houston home, and how to use the results.

What the General Inspector Does and Does Not Do

Texas home inspectors are licensed by TREC and follow a standards-of-practice checklist. For the roof, they report visible deficiencies and whether they walked it. Many do not walk it for safety or liability reasons, and the standards do not require them to. They will not do a hail count, they will not lift shingles to check seal, and most do not spend long in the attic. Their report tells you whether there is an obvious problem. It does not tell you how many years of life are left or what a replacement will cost.

What a Roofer's Pre-Purchase Inspection Adds

  • Age estimate and remaining life. From granule loss, shingle flexibility, and product identification. See how long a roof lasts in Texas.
  • Storm damage assessment. Whether there is unrepaired hail or wind damage that could be a claim for the seller now or an uninsurable problem for you later.
  • Flashing and penetration check. The things that actually leak. See common causes of roof leaks.
  • Attic inspection. Past leaks, ventilation, decking condition, evidence of previous repairs.
  • Layer count. Whether there is one layer or two, which affects replacement cost by thousands.
  • A replacement or repair quote you can use in negotiations.

Why It Matters Especially in Houston

  • Insurance. Texas carriers are increasingly refusing to write policies on roofs over 15 to 20 years old, or only offering actual cash value coverage. If you cannot insure the home, you cannot get a mortgage. Find out before you are under contract with a closing date. See roof age and Texas insurance.
  • Storm history. Houston has had hail or hurricane events almost every year. A roof that was "replaced after Harvey" may have been a cheap storm-chaser job.
  • Flipped homes. Renovated interiors with original roofs are very common in Houston flips. The kitchen is new. The roof is 22 years old.
  • Builder-grade roofs. Houses from 2005 to 2015 often have the cheapest shingle the builder could buy, installed fast. Many are failing early.

What to Ask the Seller

  • Roof age and the installer, with the invoice if they have it
  • Any insurance claims on the roof, and whether the work was done
  • Whether the warranty is transferable. See roof warranties explained.
  • The seller's disclosure, which in Texas must list known roof defects and prior leaks

Using the Inspection in Negotiation

If the roof needs replacement, you have options during the option period:

  • Ask the seller to replace it before closing, with a contractor and product you approve. Be specific or you will get the cheapest possible roof.
  • Ask for a price reduction or closing credit for the replacement cost, and do it yourself after closing with your own roofer. This is usually the better outcome.
  • Ask the seller to file a claim if there is documented storm damage, and have the work done before closing.
  • Walk away if the roof problem is large and the seller will not move.

A written inspection with photos and a quote makes all of these conversations much easier for your agent. See how much a roof costs in Houston for context on the numbers.

Timing

Schedule the roof inspection in the first few days of your option period so there is time to negotiate. Many roofing companies, including ours, can get out within a day or two for buyers under contract.
