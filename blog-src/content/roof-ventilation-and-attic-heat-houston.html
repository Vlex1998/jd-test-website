On a Houston summer afternoon, the inside of an attic can get far hotter than the air outside. That heat bakes your shingles from below, makes your AC work harder, and can shorten the life of your roof. Good ventilation is one of the most overlooked parts of a healthy roof.

How Attic Ventilation Works

A balanced system has two parts:

  • Intake vents low on the roof, usually in the soffits under the eaves, that let cooler outside air in.
  • Exhaust vents high on the roof, such as ridge vents, box vents, or turbines, that let hot, moist air out.

Hot air rises and exits at the top, pulling fresh air in at the bottom. Without enough intake, exhaust vents cannot do their job. Without enough exhaust, heat and humidity get trapped.

Why Ventilation Matters in Houston

  • Heat damage to shingles. Excessive attic heat can speed up the aging of asphalt shingles, leading to curling and granule loss.
  • Higher energy bills. A hot attic radiates heat into your living space and makes your AC run longer, especially if ductwork runs through the attic, as it does in many Houston homes.
  • Moisture and mold. Houston's humidity, plus moisture from bathrooms and kitchens, can condense in a poorly ventilated attic. That leads to mold, rotted decking, and damp insulation.
  • Warranty requirements. Many shingle manufacturers require proper ventilation for full warranty coverage.

How Much Ventilation Do You Need?

Building codes generally call for a minimum amount of net free ventilation area based on the attic floor area. A common rule is 1 square foot of ventilation for every 150 square feet of attic floor, or 1 for every 300 when the system is balanced between intake and exhaust. A roofer can calculate what your attic needs based on its size and your current vents.

Signs Your Attic Is Poorly Ventilated

  • Upstairs rooms that stay hot even when the AC runs constantly
  • Shingles that curl, blister, or lose granules earlier than expected
  • Dark stains, mold, or musty smells in the attic
  • Rusty nails or damp spots on the underside of the decking
  • Soffit vents blocked by insulation or paint

Common Ventilation Mistakes

  • Mixing exhaust types. Combining ridge vents with box vents or turbines on the same roof can short-circuit airflow, pulling air from the wrong place.
  • Blocked soffits. Blown-in insulation often covers soffit vents. Baffles keep the airflow path open.
  • Bath fans venting into the attic. All exhaust fans should vent outside the house.
  • Too little intake. Adding more exhaust without enough intake does not help.

Other Ways to Cut Attic Heat

Ventilation works best combined with good insulation on the attic floor and, in some homes, a radiant barrier. Reflective or lighter-colored shingles can help too. See energy-efficient roofing for Houston heat.
